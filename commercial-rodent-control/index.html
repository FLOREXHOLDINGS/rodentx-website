Commercial / Industrial /
Wineries & Breweries.
Built for business.

Rodent and wildlife programs for businesses that can’t afford a sighting, a shutdown, or a repeat. Documented, discreet, and sealed.

What we cover

  • Restaurants, retail, offices, warehouses, industrial sites
  • Wineries & breweries — production, storage, and tasting spaces
  • Multi-unit and property-managed buildings

The sectors differ; the pressure points repeat. Food service lives on deliveries, dry storage, and doors that open all day. Warehouses live on dock doors and racking against exterior walls. Wineries and breweries sit in open country — barns, barrel rooms, and crush pads surrounded by the habitat the animals come from, with harvest season raising the pressure every year. Multi-unit buildings add shared wall voids and utility chases, where one unit’s problem becomes the building’s. A programme is built from the building’s own survey, not from a sector template.

What you get

  • Entry-point survey and sealing plan, in writing
  • Documented findings, seals, and follow-up — a programme file per site
  • Monitoring cadence agreed before work
  • The same X Standard: Inspect / Remove / Exclude / Monitor

For a business, the documentation is not paperwork for its own sake. Wildlife and rodent findings surface in health inspections, brand audits, lease discussions, and insurance conversations. The programme file is the answer in each of those rooms: what was found, what was done, what is being monitored, and when it was last checked. It is also what makes the programme transferable inside your own organisation — a new facilities manager, a new tenant, or a new owner inherits the file and the building’s history with it, not a verbal account of what a previous contractor may or may not have done.

How it works

Every commercial job runs on a cadence: an entry-point survey first, a written scope before any work, the work itself on an agreed schedule, then documented checks on the monitoring cadence agreed up front. Service is planned around operating hours. The programme is discreet by design — the work should not be the show your customers see.

Wildlife, not only rodents

Commercial buildings draw the full list, not only mice and rats. Bats colonise the roof voids of churches, schools, and warehouses — large, quiet, undisturbed spaces close to the ground they feed over. Birds nest in vents, signage, and loading-dock structures. Raccoons work waste areas and find their way from dumpsters to roofline entries. Groundhogs burrow at the foundations of outbuildings and under slabs and porches. A commercial programme covers the building as a whole: the survey reads for wildlife as well as rodents, and the sealing plan closes the entries for both.
