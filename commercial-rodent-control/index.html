Commercial
Rodent Control.
Documented. Recurring.

Mice and rats are a compliance problem for a business, not just a nuisance. RodentX runs commercial rodent programmes the way inspectors and auditors read them: surveyed, sealed, monitored on a schedule, and documented in writing.

Built for inspection day

A commercial rodent programme has to produce paper, not just results. Every RodentX programme is documented: written findings from the initial survey, the exclusion work performed and where, the monitoring points in use, and a written report of each scheduled visit. When a health inspector, a brand auditor, or a landlord asks what is being done about rodents, the answer is a file — not a shrug.

Signs in a commercial building

The signs are the residential signs at commercial scale, and they are usually found by staff before they are reported: droppings in dry storage or along dock walls, gnawed packaging and product, rub marks at door corners and along pipe runs, nesting in stored material, and odor in closed storage rooms. In food businesses there is a second category of evidence — audit findings and inspector observations, which often record the sign before anyone names the problem. A programme starts by reading all of it: the building keeps a record of its rodent pressure, and the survey is how it gets read.

Restaurants & food service

Kitchens, dry stores, and delivery doors are rodent pressure points. The programme covers entry-point sealing around doors, docks, and utility penetrations, harborage reduction guidance for storage areas, and scheduled monitoring with inspection-ready logs. Service is planned around your hours — the work should never be the show your customers see.

Warehouses & food storage

Dock doors that don’t seal, pallet racking against exterior walls, and stored product near the floor are where warehouse rodent problems start. We survey the building shell and the storage layout, seal the shell, and monitor dock, wall, and storage zones on an agreed cadence, with the monitoring map kept in your file.

Farms, wineries & breweries

Around the Finger Lakes, barns, barrel rooms, crush pads, and tasting rooms sit in the middle of rodent habitat — grain, fruit, and old timber construction. Harvest season raises the pressure. The programme is built around the building’s calendar: survey and exclusion works scheduled between your busy periods, monitoring through them, and written reports your team can act on.

Property managers & rentals

For multi-unit and rental portfolios, rodents are a turnover and habitability issue. We run turnover inspections, proof the recurring entry points — sill gaps, utility chases, shared wall voids — and monitor on a cadence agreed per building, with findings documented per unit and per building.

Seasonal pressure

Commercial rodent pressure is not level through the year. Autumn is the peak: field rodents move toward buildings as temperatures fall and harvest takes their cover, and a warehouse or barn that was quiet in August can be active by October. Food businesses feel a second pressure in their own busy seasons, when deliveries, open doors, and stored volume all rise together. Programmes are scheduled with the building’s calendar in mind — survey and exclusion work in the quieter periods, monitoring carried through the peaks.

What the survey examines

The commercial survey covers the shell and the operation together. The shell: dock and personnel doors and their sweeps, utility penetrations, vents, roofline, and the junctions between building sections and additions. The operation: where food and waste are stored and how they move, what sits against exterior walls, where pallets and product rest, and which doors stand open through the day. Most commercial rodent problems are a shell problem and an operations problem at once — an open dock door beside a dumpster is both. The findings separate the two, because the fixes are different and both are needed.

How the programme works

  • Survey — entry points, harborage, and pressure, in writing
  • Exclude — the building shell sealed with durable, animal-proof materials, every seal listed
  • Remove — the animals inside are trapped and removed as part of the programme
  • Monitor — scheduled checks on the cadence agreed up front, with a written report each visit

The programme is exclusion-first: it is built on sealing, trapping, and monitoring. If you searched for commercial pest control, this is the rodent part of that programme — run by a wildlife company, and built to keep rodents out rather than treat the building on repeat.
