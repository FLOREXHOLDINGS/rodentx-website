Finger Lakes wildlife beyond the attic — yard, crawl space, chimney, and vent animals, handled species-correctly.

What we cover

  • Groundhogs — burrows near foundations, sheds, and gardens
  • Skunks — denning under porches, sheds, and crawl spaces
  • Opossums — crawl space, garage, and outbuilding visitors
  • Birds — vent, chimney, and soffit nesting

Groundhogs

A groundhog burrow is a system, not a hole — a main entrance, usually a secondary exit, and chambers below. Against a foundation, the digging removes support soil and channels water toward the wall. Under a shed or porch it undermines the base. Groundhogs return to established burrows and their young inherit them, so a burrow filled in spring is re-opened by summer unless the animals are removed and the site is defended below grade.

Skunks

Skunks den where the digging is easy and the cover is close: under porches, decks, sheds, and crawl space edges. A denning skunk is a nightly presence, not a visitor — the entry is dug, the ground at it is worn, and the odor is a fact of the address. Skunks also dig lawns for grubs, which is a different and lesser problem. The inspection distinguishes a den from a passer-by, because the answers differ.

Opossums

Opossums are opportunists: a crawl space, a garage, or an outbuilding left open is shelter taken without modification. They rarely damage a structure the way the other animals on this page do — the issue is presence, droppings, and the occasional animal that dies in the space it entered. Removal and the closing of the access point settle most opossum problems completely.

Birds

Birds nest in vents, chimneys, and soffit openings — warm, sheltered, and above ground predators. A nest in a bathroom or dryer vent blocks the vent it occupies; nesting material and droppings accumulate at the opening. The work is removal of the birds and the nesting, then a closure that keeps birds out while the vent keeps working.

What we do

  • Species-correct removal, matched to the animal and the site
  • Entry and harborage sealing
  • Prevention for the next season, set out in the findings

Burrow Defense

Groundhogs and skunks denning under porches, decks, and sheds are stopped below grade with Burrow Defense: a trench dug one foot deep and one foot out around the base, a steel mesh barrier set in the trench, then reburied.

Questions

The inspection confirms the burrow system and its exits. The animals are removed, and the standard prevention is Burrow Defense — a steel mesh barrier buried a foot deep and a foot out around the base, so the next groundhog cannot dig in where the last one did. Filling the hole alone does not hold; the barrier is the point.
A passing skunk leaves tracks and maybe a night of odor. A denning skunk leaves a used entry — a dug opening at a porch or shed base, worn ground, and recurring odor at the same place. The inspection reads the entry: an active den has a pattern a passer-by does not leave.
The birds and the nesting are removed, and then the vent has to be closed correctly — a cover that excludes birds while the vent still does its job. Nesting material left in a vent is a blockage and, at a dryer vent, a hazard, so the cleanout is part of the work.
