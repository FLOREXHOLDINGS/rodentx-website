Squirrels in Attic.
Daytime noise, chewed entry.

Squirrels chew entries, nest in insulation and soffits, and return to the same hole. We find the entry, remove, and seal it properly.

Signs of squirrels in the attic

Squirrels announce themselves. The signs are daytime noise, a visible entry, and the damage both leave behind.

  • Scratching, running, or gnawing in the attic or soffits during the day
  • A chewed entry hole in fascia, soffit, or roofline — often at a corner or a roof joint
  • Nesting material — leaves, twigs, and shredded insulation gathered in one area
  • Droppings in the attic, and insulation torn or tunnelled
  • Squirrels seen on the roof, at the eaves, or entering the same spot repeatedly

The timing of the noise is the first diagnostic. Daytime activity points to squirrels; night activity points elsewhere. Tell us when you hear it and where, and the inspection starts from there.

How squirrels use a house

The gray squirrel treats an attic as a hollow tree with better weather. It comes and goes through one main entry — a hole it chewed itself or a gap it widened — and it uses the attic for shelter and for raising young, generally in spring and again in late summer. The same animal uses the same entry out of habit, and other squirrels use it after.

Squirrels gnaw constantly. In the wild that wears down teeth on wood and nut shells; in an attic it is applied to fascia, soffits, stored items, and wiring. The entry hole also grows — a gap that starts as a gnawed edge becomes an opening other animals can use. An active squirrel entry is not a stable situation. It widens.

How squirrels reach the roof

Squirrels do not need an invitation, but they do need a route. Overhanging limbs are the common one — a branch within jumping distance of the roof is a road. Utility lines and the corners of siding are also climbed routinely, and a squirrel on the roof tests what it finds there: vent edges, trim joints, and any wood already softened by weather. This is why the inspection looks at the trees and the roofline together, and why prevention starts with the limbs. A sealed entry on a roof the squirrels can still reach invites a new hole beside the old one; the route and the entry are dealt with as one problem.

What we do

  • Entry location — the main entry and any secondary gaps found and mapped
  • Removal — the squirrels removed, matched to the building and the season
  • Sealing — the entry and the secondary gaps closed with durable, animal-proof materials
  • Cleanout options — nesting material and affected insulation dealt with where needed
  • Monitoring — a follow-up check that the entry stays shut and the noise stops

Removal without sealing is a short reprieve. The entry is the problem; the animal is the symptom. Every squirrel job therefore ends with the seal, listed in writing.

The damage pattern

An established squirrel attic shows a pattern. Insulation is tunnelled and gathered into nests, often in the same corner season after season. Stored items near the nest are gnawed. Droppings collect along the runs. The entry itself is the most consequential damage: chewed edges are worked at repeatedly, the hole grows, and a grown hole admits weather as well as animals — water at the roofline rots the wood around the entry, which makes the next entry easier to chew. Dealt with early, a squirrel job is an entry and a seal. Left for seasons, it becomes carpentry, insulation work, and a colony that regards the attic as established territory.

What we seal

The chewed entry itself, soffits and fascia, roofline gaps, vent covers, and any secondary entries found on inspection — each one listed in writing, with the materials used. Where a roofline section has been chewed through, repair guidance is part of the findings.

What you get in writing

The entry points found, what was removed, what was sealed and with what, and any follow-up. If the attic insulation has been nested in or tunnelled, the restoration options are set out as a separate step. See Attic Restoration →.

Prevention

Tree limbs cut back from the roofline remove the squirrel’s road to the roof. Vents stay covered and intact. Trim and fascia kept in repair deny the gnawing a starting edge. None of it is elaborate — it removes access, and access is the whole game. If daytime noise starts again, note where it is loudest and call while the entry is still small. A gnawed edge found in its first weeks is an hour’s sealing; the same edge after two seasons of litters is a repair.

Wildlife removal vs. pest control

People often search for pest control when they find bats or squirrels, but this is wildlife work, not a spray treatment. No treatment fixes an open entry, and these animals are handled under wildlife rules, not pesticide labels. RodentX is a wildlife company: inspection, humane removal, and exclusion — the seal — are the service. If your problem is an animal in the building, this page is the right one.

Questions

Squirrels are daytime animals. Scratching, running, and gnawing heard in the attic or soffits during the day — and quiet at night — points to squirrels. Noise at night points to a nocturnal animal instead, which is why the timing of the noise is one of the first things we ask about.
Not reliably. A squirrel using an attic has shelter and a nesting site, and it returns to the same entry out of habit. Some leave seasonally and come back. The entry has to be found, the animals removed, and the opening sealed — in that order.
Squirrels gnaw constantly — it is how they manage teeth that never stop growing. In an attic, what gets gnawed is what is there: wood, stored items, and wiring. Gnawed wiring is one of the general risks of leaving squirrels in place, and it is part of what the inspection checks.
Squirrels raise young in attics, generally in spring and again in late summer. Where young are present, the removal plan accounts for them — the timing and the method are explained before work begins, the same as any other seasonal restriction.
Every entry and every roofline is different, so there is no flat answer that could be published honestly. We inspect first, then give you the scope in writing before work begins — the entry found, what will be sealed, and what happens next.
