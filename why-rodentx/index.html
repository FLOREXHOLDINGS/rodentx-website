Why RodentX.
Same X. Higher standard.

RodentX closed at the end of August 2023. It has been rebuilt since — as a company, not only as a service. This page is the record of both: what the standard is, and what happened.

The X Standard

The rebuild runs on the X Standard: the same four moves, in the same order, on every job. The X is not decoration — it is the seal, the closure, the proof the job is finished.

  • 01 / Inspect — Find the entry, the run, and the damage. Written findings before work.
  • 02 / Remove — Humane, lawful removal matched to the species and the season.
  • 03 / Exclude — The seal. Durable, animal-proof materials — the X that means it stays shut.
  • 04 / Monitor — Check the seal holds and the activity stops.

What happened in 2023.

RodentX stopped operating at the end of August 2023. The stop was abrupt, and it was mine. RodentX was a one-man company — I was the office, the phone, and the ladder. When I stopped, nobody was left to answer, and some customers were left without a reply. The reviews from that period say so, and they are not wrong about the silence.

The time since has been spent learning what I did not know then — about running a company, not just doing the work. The steps to reopen properly were taken deliberately, not quickly. This RodentX is built so the work stands on a system, not on one man’s day: documented inspections, written findings, exclusion reviewed before service begins, monitoring after. Same name. Same X. A company that answers.

What has not changed

The work itself was never the failure. Finding an entry the size of a pencil line in a century-old roofline, reading an attic, sealing a building so the work holds — that craft is the same, and it is the reason the name is worth clearing rather than replacing. What failed in 2023 was the company around the craft: the answering, the structure, the follow-through. That distinction is the rebuild in one sentence. The skills were hired back; the structure was built new. A customer of the first RodentX who recognised the work would recognise it again. What they would not recognise is a job without a file, a finding given from memory, or a question left without an answer — and that is the point.

What the rebuild changed

The first RodentX was a man and a ladder — skilled at the work, and the whole company at once. The rebuild keeps the work and changes the structure around it. Every job now runs on the X Standard and produces a file: the findings, the seals and their materials, what was removed, and the monitoring result. The file exists whether the job is a single attic or a multi-site programme, and it belongs to the customer’s record as much as to ours. If the animal is back, or a seal is questioned, the answer comes from the file — not from whoever happens to answer the phone.

The service area is the same region, served whole: the Finger Lakes, with no single town as the base. The name is the same. The way the company is run is the change being offered as evidence. This page exists because the account belongs in the open, where a customer deciding whether to call can read it before they decide — the same standard of documentation the company now applies to a roofline, applied to itself.

What you get in writing

Findings, what was removed, what was sealed and with what, and any follow-up. Some exclusion work carries a warranty — coverage for the property is reviewed with you before service begins.

The written record is a discipline, not a courtesy. It is what allows a warranty conversation to be specific, a monitoring check to be verified against a list, and a customer two years later to establish exactly what was done at their building. Companies that run on memory cannot offer any of that.
