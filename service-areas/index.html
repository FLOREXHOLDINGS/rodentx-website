One Region.
Shaped by Its Lakes.

Finger Lakes Region, NY. One region, served whole — every area on this page carries the same standard, and none of them is the base.

RodentX is a service-area business: there is no office to visit and no street address published, because the work happens at your building. The region served is the Finger Lakes — the towns around the lakes and the corridors between them, from Rochester in the west to Syracuse in the east, Ithaca in the south to the lake shores in the north. The areas below organise the region for routing and for the city pages. They are not tiers. A job in Yates County gets the same standard, the same documentation, and the same follow-up as a job in Rochester.

Areas served

  • Rochester — Western Finger Lakes hub. Includes the Rochester area.
  • Canandaigua–Geneva — Lakes corridor hub. Includes Canandaigua, Geneva, Seneca Lake.
  • Syracuse — Eastern hub. Includes the Syracuse area and Auburn.
  • Ithaca — Southern hub. Includes the Ithaca area and Cayuga Lake.
  • Yates County — Includes Penn Yan, Dundee, Naples, and Keuka Lake.

The Corning–Elmira edge — the Southern Tier edge of the region. Includes Elmira, Corning, and Painted Post.

Smaller towns are served inside these areas. The listed areas are the core of the region — not its limit. If your town sits beyond the areas above, ask: surrounding areas are handled on request, and the answer will be plain either way. Tell us your town on the form, and we will confirm coverage by phone or email before anything is scheduled.

How coverage works

Coverage is confirmed per job, from the form or the call: the town, the animal, and the building. That confirmation is deliberate. A service-area business that claims everywhere serves nowhere carefully, so the region is stated plainly and the edges are answered honestly. Within the region, routing follows the job — inspections and programme visits are scheduled by area so that follow-up and monitoring run on the cadence agreed, not on whoever is passing through.

The region’s building stock is part of the work: nineteenth-century farmhouses and village homes with layered rooflines, lake cottages opened and closed with the seasons, barns and winery buildings in open country, college-town rentals in Ithaca, and the commercial corridors of Rochester and Syracuse. The areas differ in housing more than in wildlife — the same bats, squirrels, raccoons, and rodents work the whole region — which is why one standard, documented the same way, serves all of them.
