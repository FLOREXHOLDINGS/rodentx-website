Prevention checklist

  • Seal. Keep entries, vents, and gaps sealed — the seal is the point.
  • Store. Store food and waste so it does not feed harborage.
  • Clear. Clear harborage — what to clear, seal, and store differently, per your inspection findings.
  • Watch. Watch for the signs: scratching, droppings, gnaw marks, odor, torn vents.

Prevention follows your inspection findings — the checklist above is general guidance, not a substitute for an inspection.

Prevention, in more depth

Seal — and know what sealing means. Animals use gaps the size of the animal: a quarter inch for a mouse, three-eighths of an inch for a bat, a gnawed edge for a squirrel, a light vent cover for a raccoon. A yearly walk of the building with those sizes in mind finds most entries while they are still small.

Store — food, waste, and the shelter of clutter. Waste in sealed containers, pet food and seed in closed bins, storage lifted off floors — rodents need food and cover together, and storage discipline removes both.

Clear — the cover against the building. Vegetation, woodpiles, and clutter against a foundation hide entries from view. Limbs overhanging the roof are the squirrels’ road and one of the raccoons’. Clearing keeps the building readable.

Watch — and write it down. The first sign of a return is usually a sound. A dated note — the night, the place, what it sounded like — turns it into evidence an inspection can start from.

Seasonal notes

Autumn is the pressure season: rodents move toward buildings ahead of the cold. Spring is denning season, when removals are planned around young. Summer is bat maternity season, roughly May through mid-August, when exclusion waits and inspections plan.
